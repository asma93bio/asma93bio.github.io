Final Year Project

In Silico Evaluation of Natural Saponins and Phytoestrogens as Multi-Target Inhibitors in Breast Cancer
M.S. Thesis, University of Algiers 1 Benyoucef Benkhedda · Defended with Honors (Mention Excellent), 2025 · Presented at SPDFS-2025

4 compounds screened 3 protein targets PyRx · AutoDock Vina · Discovery Studio
Why This Project

Standard endocrine therapy for ER+ breast cancer (tamoxifen) targets a single receptor — 30 to 40% of patients develop resistance to it within 2 to 5 years, because cancer cells reroute through compensatory pathways when only one target is blocked. This project tested whether hitting Target 1 alongside two resistance-driving pathways (Target 2 and Target 3) at once could close that escape route.

Objective

Address treatment resistance in estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer by identifying natural compounds capable of disrupting multiple survival pathways at once, rather than a single target.

Approach

Multi-target molecular docking of four natural saponins against Target 1, Target 2, and Target 3, benchmarked against reference pharmacological compounds (Reference 1, Reference 2, Reference 3).

Key Result

The lead candidate outperformed or matched all three reference drugs across all three targets — evidence for a multi-pathway alternative to single-target endocrine therapy.

Challenges Solved
  • ADMET prediction on heavy compounds: the saponins' high molecular weight caused SMILES-based errors in SwissADME, the tool most researchers default to. Switched to ADMETlab 3.0, which handles high-MW compounds more reliably.
  • Grid box configuration: PyRx's docking engine (AutoDock Vina) requires precise definition of the search grid around the binding site — an imprecise grid silently produces misleading results, so each target's active site was carefully mapped before docking.
  • Ligand preparation: proper preparation of each molecule (protonation state, energy minimization) before docking, to ensure the binding poses generated were chemically valid.
  • Affinity vs. bioavailability trade-off: the strongest binders also violated 3 of 4 Lipinski rules (high molecular weight, excess H-bond donors/acceptors) — meaning high potency doesn't guarantee oral bioavailability. Proposed liposomal nanocarrier encapsulation as a delivery strategy to bypass this limitation.
Limitations & Next Steps
  • In silico only — binding affinities are predicted, not experimentally validated; the next step would be in vitro binding assays (SPR or ITC) on the lead candidate specifically to confirm the docking prediction.
  • Static docking, no dynamics — the poses reflect a single snapshot, not how the complex behaves over time; molecular dynamics simulation (e.g. GROMACS) would test whether the binding is stable, not just favorable on paper.
  • Natural compound purification — the lead candidate would need to be isolated in sufficient quantity and purity from its natural source before any further testing, a separate bottleneck from the computational result.
